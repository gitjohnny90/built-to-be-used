What makes a group game actually work

  • Materials kill momentum. The best group games need nothing but paper and a pen. If it needs props, half the room waits while someone hunts for them.
  • Tiered difficulty keeps everyone in. Mixed groups need easy wins for newcomers and hard rounds for the deacon who's read the Word forty times.
  • Cited answers end disputes. Bible trivia without chapter-and-verse citations turns into a debate. With them, someone just looks it up.
  • Icebreakers should be askable by anyone. The good ones work for the first-time visitor and the founding member alike.
