The home maintenance schedule nobody hands you at closing

Most expensive home repairs started as cheap problems nobody noticed. A clogged gutter becomes a foundation issue; a $6 water heater anode becomes a $1,400 replacement. The fix isn't being handy — it's having a schedule.

Five checks most first-time homeowners miss

  • Find your shutoffs before you need them. Water main, gas valve, electrical panel. At 2 a.m. with water pouring through a ceiling, you don't want to be learning the basement layout.
  • Change HVAC filters every 1–3 months. It's the single cheapest thing you can do for the most expensive system you own.
  • Flush the water heater once a year. Sediment buildup is the main reason tanks die early — and the flush takes about 30 minutes.
  • Walk your roofline every spring and fall. From the ground, with binoculars. You're looking for lifted shingles, sagging gutters, and flashing gaps.
  • Test the sump pump before the wet season. Pour in a bucket of water. If it doesn't kick on, you found out the easy way.
