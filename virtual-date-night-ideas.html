13 virtual date night ideas for long distance couples — all free, all runnable tonight

"We should call this week" is not a date. A date has a time you both protected, a thing you're doing together, and a little anticipation beforehand. None of that requires being in the same room — or spending a dollar. Here are thirteen virtual dates you could run tonight with nothing but your phones and a free evening.

One rule before the ideas: do something, don't just talk

Open-ended video calls drift into status reports — work, weather, "anyway, I should sleep." The dates below all work because the conversation has something to orbit. You'll talk more, not less, when you're both looking at the same painting or burning the same garlic.

The ideas

  • 1. Cook the same meal on camera. Pick one recipe you both have ingredients for (eggs and toast count), prop your phones in your kitchens, and cook it together in real time. Eat "across the table" when you're done. Disasters are better than successes here.
  • 2. Tour a world-class museum from your couches. Google Arts & Culture offers free virtual walkthroughs of museums like the Van Gogh Museum and the Musée d'Orsay. Share a screen, wander the galleries, and each pick one piece you'd hang in your future home — then defend it.
  • 3. Run a synced movie night the low-tech way. You don't need a watch-party extension: count down from three and press play together, then stay on a voice call with mics on. Pick the snack in advance so you're both eating the same thing.
  • 4. Play the 36 questions game. Search "36 questions that lead to love" (the free, famous study set) and take turns answering one question each. You won't finish the list in one night — that's the point. Bookmark where you stopped.
  • 5. Give a Street View tour of your hometown. Share your screen, open Google Maps, and walk your partner down the streets you grew up on — your school, the corner store, the spot where the embarrassing story happened. Then trade. It's the closest thing to meeting each other's past.
  • 6. Stargaze on the phone. Both step outside (or to a window), call each other, and just look up while you talk. If your time zones don't share a night sky, the person in daylight gets to hear the other one describe it. Free astronomy apps can tell you what you're looking at.
  • 7. Read aloud to each other. Pick a book — anything from a thriller to a children's classic — and trade chapters over a call, one of you reading while the other lies there and listens. A chapter a week becomes a ritual that outlasts the distance.
  • 8. Host a two-person game night. Free options are everywhere: online chess, checkers, or drawing-guessing games playable in any browser with a private room code. Best of five, loser plans the next date. Trash talk is encouraged.
  • 9. Plan the next visit in obsessive detail. Open a shared note and build the itinerary for the next time you're together — the restaurant, the hike, the lazy morning. Even if the date isn't booked yet, planning it makes it real. This one doubles as a hope dispenser on hard weeks.
  • 10. Trade playlists, then listen together. Each of you builds a short playlist for the other — "songs that sound like the week I had" or "songs from the year I turned sixteen." Press play at the same time and explain each track as it comes. You will learn things no questionnaire would surface.
  • 11. Run a pantry bake-off. Each of you makes a dessert using only what's already in the kitchen, on camera, with a 45-minute timer. Present your creations like a cooking show finale and score each other on taste (claimed), looks, and degree of difficulty.
  • 12. Do a sunset swap. If you're in different time zones, one of you video-calls the other during your sunset, then you return the favor at yours. It turns the time difference — usually the enemy — into the actual date.
  • 13. Write letters live. Spend twenty minutes of a call writing each other a real paper letter, in comfortable silence, then mail them the next day. The call is the date; the envelope that shows up days later is the encore. Mail still out-romances every app ever made.

How to make any of these feel like a real date

  • Book it like one. A planned hour at a protected time feels like an event; a spontaneous call feels like a check-in. Put it on both calendars with a name ("Thursday: bake-off").
  • Show up like one. Change out of the work hoodie. It sounds silly and it changes the whole evening.
  • End with the next one booked. The post-call emptiness is real. The antidote is hanging up already knowing when you'll see each other next — on-screen or off.
