Record keeping for chicken keepers who want real numbers

Every flock keeper eventually gets asked what their eggs really cost. Without records, the honest answer is "no idea." With records, you know which hen stopped laying, when feed prices crept up, and whether this month's dip is molt or a problem.

Numbers worth tracking in a backyard flock

  • Eggs per week per hen. First-year layers of good breeds typically give 4–6 a week. A sudden drop with no season change means stress, parasites, hidden nests, or illness.
  • Feed cost per dozen. Track feed purchases against egg counts and you'll know your real cost — usually an eye-opener in both directions.
  • Brooder temps by week. Start ~95°F, drop 5°F per week. Chick behavior tells you the truth: huddling means cold, edges mean hot.
  • Health observations with dates. "She seemed off last Tuesday, or maybe Wednesday" is no help to an avian vet. Dated notes are.
  • Predator-proofing checks each season. Half-inch hardware cloth, dig aprons, two-step latches — and re-check after every near miss.
